The process of coordinating work activities so that they are completed efficiently and effectively with and through other people. The process represents the ongoing functions or primary activities engaged in by managers. These functions are typically labeled planning, organizing, leading, and controlling

  1. How do managers contribute to the success of an organization?

Managers engage in many short, varied activities and frequently switch between tasks (Henry Mintzberg, John Kotter). They spend most of their time interacting with people—employees, supervisors, and external stakeholders. Around two-thirds of their time is communication (meetings, calls, conversations). Managers rarely work alone and often handle interruptions and unexpected issues.

Their role includes:

Gathering information

Building relationships

Solving problems

Coordinating work

Communication is the key tool for guiding actions and ensuring smooth operations. Managers achieve top performance by being active, adaptable, and highly connected through effective communication.

  1. What are the roles that managers play in organizations?

The Roles Managers Play (Mintzberg’s Roles)

Managers perform three main types of roles in organizations: interpersonal, informational, and decisional.

1. Interpersonal Roles
These involve interacting with people and building relationships. Managers act as:

Figurehead: perform ceremonial and symbolic duties.

Leader: motivate, guide, and manage employees.

Liaison: build networks and maintain contacts inside and outside the organization.

2. Informational Roles
Managers handle and share information. They act as:

Monitor: gather and analyze information from the environment.

Disseminator: share important information with employees.

Spokesperson: communicate information to external stakeholders and the public.

3. Decisional Roles

Managers make important organizational decisions. They act as:

Entrepreneur: initiate change and innovation.

Disturbance Handler: respond to crises and unexpected problems.

Resource Allocator: decide how to distribute limited resources.

Negotiator: handle negotiations with employees, suppliers, and other parties.

Overall, managers use these roles together to lead, communicate, and make decisions that help organizations achieve their goals.

  1. What Varies in a Manager’s Job?

Why do managerial jobs differ from one manager to another?

The manager’s job is always changing, but two roles are becoming especially more important:

  • The emphasis in a manager’s job changes based on the environment and organizational needs, with certain roles becoming more important over time.
  • Entrepreneurial Role (Increasing Importance)
      • Identify threats (competition, technological changes)
      • Recognize opportunities (new markets, innovation, improvements)
  • Leadership Role (Growing Significance)
      • Be proactive and adaptable to stay competitive
      • Act as mentors and strategic leaders, not just supervisors
      • Attract, train, motivate, and retain talented employees

Modern managers must be both innovative (entrepreneurial) and people-focused (leadership-oriented) to help organizations achieve and sustain high performance.

  1. How do managerial responsibilities contribute to achieving organizational goals?

Managers are responsible for planning, organizing, directing, and controlling organizational activities. Their work includes nine main types of responsibilities:

Long-range planning: Involved in strategic planning and development.

Controlling: Evaluate and take corrective action on human, financial, and material resources.

Environmental scanning: Monitor changes and business indicators.

Supervision: oversee subordinates’ work.

Coordinating: Coordinate work within and outside the team.

Customer relations and marketing: Direct contact with customers and potential customers.

Community relations: Maintain relationships with external groups (agencies, civic groups, suppliers).
Internal consulting: Use technical expertise to solve internal problems, acting as inside consultants for organizational change and development.

Monitoring products and services: Plan, schedule, and monitor production and delivery..
