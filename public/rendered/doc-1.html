1. What are the basic characteristics of managerial decision-making?

Managerial decision-making involves identifying, evaluating, and selecting the best option among alternatives. It is a continuous and essential process at all organizational levels. Top managers make strategic decisions affecting long-term success and organizational survival.

Lower-level managers make operational decisions related to daily activities and departmental performance. Decisions influence stakeholders such as employees, customers, and shareholders. Even small decisions can affect productivity, costs, employee morale, and employee turnover. Managerial decision-making is important because it directly impacts organizational effectiveness, performance, and long-term success.

  1. What are the two systems of decision-making in the brain?

The brain uses two main systems in decision-making: Reflective and Reactive (Reflexive)

Reflective System: Logical, slow, and analytical

Involves careful thinking, planning, and evaluation

Used for reasoning and detailed analysis

Controlled by the prefrontal cortex

Reactive (Reflexive) System: Fast, automatic, and intuitive

Based on emotions, habits, and past experience

Used for quick decisions in urgent situations

Controlled by basal ganglia and amygdala

Both systems work together depending on the situation.

  1. What is the difference between programmed and nonprogrammed decisions?

Programmed Decisions

Programmed decisions are routine, repeated, and structured decisions.

    • They are made in regular situations that happen again and again
    • They follow rules, procedures, or guidelines
    • The decision criteria are known or can be predicted

Examples:

    • Ordering raw materials based on expected production and stock levels
    • Scheduling part-time employees based on demand and availability

These decisions can be handled efficiently using established methods.

Nonprogrammed decisions

  • new, unstructured, and non-routine decisions that are made in unusual or uncertain situations.
  • They are based on unclear or incomplete information, so there are no fixed rules or procedures to follow.
  • In these situations, managers must use judgment, creativity, and careful thinking to find the best possible solution. Because they require more effort and analysis, they are also called high-involvement decisions.
  • Example :Deciding whether to adopt a new technology
    • Managers must consider:
      • Performance
      • Competition
      • Future acceptance
  • the manager must collect as much information as possible and make an informed judgment.
  1. What barriers exist that make effective decision-making difficult?

There are a number of barriers to effective decision-making. Effective managers are aware of these potential barriers and try to overcome them as much as possible.

Bounded rationality means that managers cannot make fully rational decisions, especially in complex situations.

Decision Making is limited by: limited information, limited time, and limits ability to process data.

Challenges:

Managers may not know all possible alternatives.

They may not fully understand the consequences of each option.

Information may be incomplete or too complex to analyze accurately.

Therefore, managers usually make satisfactory decisions rather than perfect ones, based on the information and resources available.

  1. Explain the six steps in the systematic decision-making process used for making better nonprogrammed decisions.

Techniques for Making Better Nonprogrammed Decisions

Nonprogrammed decisions require careful analysis and a step-by-step process.

Systematic Decision-Making Process:

Recognize the need for a decision.

Managers must identify problems and opportunities early to make effective decisions.

2. Generate multiple alternatives.

Managers should create multiple and creative options to find the best solution.

Analyze the alternatives.

Managers should carefully evaluate all options using accurate information, evidence, critical thinking, stakeholder input, and ethical consideration to choose the best long-term solution.

4. Select the best alternative.

Managers should choose the most suitable option after careful evaluation of alternatives, avoiding delay and over analysis, accepting that perfect decisions are not possible, and using input from others to reduce uncertainty and improve decision quality.

5. Implement the selected alternative.

Managers must put the chosen decision into action effectively, manage possible resistance or conflict, and ensure ethical follow-through even under pressure or disagreement.

6. Evaluate the effectiveness of the decision

Managers must evaluate the results of their decisions to determine success or failure, learn from outcomes, and improve future decision-making while avoiding repeated mistakes.

  1. What is a heuristic, and when would it be appropriate to use a heuristic for decision-making?

Heuristics (quick mental shortcuts) that allow a decision maker to reach a good decision quickly. They are strategies that develop based on prior experience.

  1. What is confirmation bias? Explain how it can be a barrier to effective decision-making?

Confirmation bias is the tendency to accept information that confirms existing beliefs while ignoring opposing evidence. It becomes a barrier to effective decision-making because it prevents people from evaluating information objectively and may lead to poor decisions.

  1. What are the two types of conflict? Which one is constructive, and which is destructive?
    • Process conflict: Disagreement about how work should be done. This can be positive and lead to better solutions. It is considered constructive conflict because it encourages discussion, improves problem-solving, and can lead to better decisions and outcomes.
    • Relationship conflict: Personal conflict between individuals. This is harmful and should be minimized. It is considered destructive conflict because it creates tension, reduces teamwork, and negatively affects performance and decision-making.

Vinyl Records Make a Comeback

The music industry has seen a series of innovations that have improved audio quality—vinyl records sales were eventually surpassed by compact discs in the 1980s, which were then eclipsed by digital music in the early 2000s. Both of the newer technologies boast superior sound quality to vinyl records. Vinyl should be dead . . . yet it’s not. Some say this is simply a result of nostalgia—people love to harken back to older times.

However, some audiophiles say that vinyl records produce a “warm” sound that can’t be reproduced in any other format. In addition, a vinyl record is a tangible product (you can feel it, touch it, and see it when you own the physical record) and is more attractive, from an aesthetic perspective, than a CD. It is also a format that encourages listening to an entire album at once, rather than just listening to individual tracks, which can change the listening experience.Whatever the reasons, vinyl is making an impressive comeback. Sales growth has been in the double digits for the last several years (over 50% in 2015 and again in 2016) and is expected to exceed $5 billion in 2017. Sony, which hasn’t produced a vinyl record since 1989, recently announced that it is back in the vinyl business. One of the biggest challenges to making vinyl records is that most of the presses are 40+ years old. In the record-making process, vinyl bits are heated to 170 degrees, and then a specialized machine exerts 150 tons of pressure to press the vinyl into the shape of the record. About a dozen new vinyl record manufacturers have sprung up in the last decade in the United States. Independent Record Pressing, a company based in New Jersey, began producing vinyl records in 2015 using old, existing presses. Their goal upon starting up was to produce over a million records a year. Even at that level of production, though, demand far outstrips the company’s capacity to produce because of the limited number of presses available. They could run their machines nonstop, 24 hours a day, and not catch up with demand.The big question is what the future holds for this industry. Will this just be a passing fad? Will the vinyl record industry remain a small niche market? Or is this the renaissance, the rebirth of a product that can withstand

the test of time and alternative technologies? If it’s a rebirth, then we should see demand continue to grow at its recent rapid pace . . . and if demand remains strong, then investing in new presses may well be worthwhile.If this is just a short-lived nostalgic return to an outdated media, however, then the large capital investment required to purchase new presses will never be recouped. Even with the recent growth, vinyl records still accounted for only 7% of overall music industry sales in 2015. That may be enough to get old presses running again, but so far it hasn’t been enough to promote a lot of investment in new machines. The cost of a new press? Almost half a million dollars. At least one manufacturer is optimistic about the future of vinyl. GZ Media, based in Czechoslovakia, is currently the world’s largest producer of vinyl records. President and owner Zdenek Pelc kept his record factory going during the lean years when vinyl sales bottomed out. He admits that the decision was not wholly logical; he continued in part because of an emotional attachment to the media. After demand for vinyl records practically disappeared, Pelc kept just a few of the presses running to meet the demand that remained. His

intention was to be the last remaining manufacturer of vinyl records. Pelc’s emotional attachment to vinyl records seems to have served him well, and it’s a great example of why basing decisions on pure logic doesn’t always lead to the best results. Consumers make purchasing decisions in part based on the emotional appeal of the product, so it shouldn’t be surprising that consumers also feel an emotional attachment to vinyl records,as Pelc did.When demand for vinyl records was low, Pelc stored the company’s presses that were no longer in use so that they could be cannibalized for parts as needed. When sales began to grow again in 2005, he started pulling old

machines out of storage and even invested in a few new ones. This has made GZ Media not only the largest vinyl record producer in the world, but also one of the only ones with new factory equipment. GZ Media produces over 20 million vinyl records a year, and Pelc is excited to continue that trend and to remain a major manufacturer in what is currently still considered a niche market.
